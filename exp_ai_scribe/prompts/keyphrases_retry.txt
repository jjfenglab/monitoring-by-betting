We are monitoring an AI scribe deployed at a hospital. We want to monitor for changes in the quality of the original draft as well as the editing behavior. To do this, we are extracting keyphrases summarizing these two aspects for each clinical note. Shifts in the frequency of particular keyphrases will indicate shifts in either the quality of the draft or the editing behavior.

The input below shows:
1. ORIGINAL AI DRAFT: the full AI-generated draft
2. EDITS: a numbered list of changes. Each edit shows:
   - BEFORE: surrounding context from the draft with <del>deleted text</del>
   - AFTER: surrounding context with <ins>inserted text</ins>

Evaluate deficiencies in the initial AI draft only based on clinician edits. Insertions alone usually indicate information that was missing from the AI draft. Deletions alone usually indicate the AI draft had unnecessary, incorrect, or unsupported content. For edits with both deletions and insertions, evaluate the semantic difference between old and new rather than treating them independently.

YOUR TASK:
Extract all concepts describing (1) the quality of the initial draft and (2) the editing behavior to this clinical note. The initial draft was from an AI scribe and then was edited by a clinician to become the final note.

Only list keyphrases that characterize was was explicitly evidenced in the clinical note. Do not infer or add details unless explicitly evidenced.

QUALITY OF INITIAL DRAFT:
Draft quality keyphrases describe WHAT WAS DEFICIENT about the AI draft that necessitated editing. Use the format <deficiency> in <content>:

## Deficiency Types
- missing: required content that was entirely absent from the draft, so edits added this in
- incomplete: content was present but lacked key details, so edits added more details
- unnecessary: language that is unnecessary for inclusion in the clinical note, so edits deleted such text
- incorrect: factually wrong information (e.g., wrong medication name, wrong diagnosis), so edits corrected this information
- verbose: unnecessarily long content without adding clinical value, so edits shortened the text

## Content Categories
Use the same UMLS-aligned categories as for edits (diagnosis, treatment plan, medication orders, procedure details, etc.)

Examples:
- "missing procedure documentation" - procedure consent/technique was entirely absent
- "incomplete medication orders" - medications listed but missing dosages
- "unnecessary problem list" - problem list in the initial draft were deemed unnecessary and were thus deleted
- "incorrect medication name" - wrong drug name corrected
- "verbose treatment discussion" - lengthy explanation that was later condensed

DESCRIBING EDITS:
Edits should be described using the format of <action> <content>:

## Actions
- added: new content inserted
- removed: content deleted
- corrected: factual error fixed (edit in clinical meaning)
- clarified: ambiguous content made more precise (edit in clinical meaning)
- condensed: content shortened without information loss (primarily a stylistic edit)
- restructured: organization or formatting changed (primarily a stylistic edit)
- edited: this is a generalization of all the prior actions, which should be used when listing generalizastions of the edit.

## Content Categories
Use UMLS-aligned clinical terminology when describing WHAT was edited, such as:
- diagnosis, differential diagnosis
- treatment plan, treatment options
- medication orders
- procedure details
- symptoms, chief complaint
- exam findings
- test orders, imaging orders
- referrals, consultations
- follow-up plan, follow-up timing
- patient instructions, discharge instructions
- note sections, note structure

Avoid:
- Too specific: "added walker use information" → use "added patient instructions"
- Too general: "clinical edits" → use "added treatment plan"

For each concept, provide:
1. Keyphrases should be no more than 3 words and follow the structure described.
2. Synonyms or alternative phrasings following the same structure.
3. Increasing generalizations that capture similar edit behaviors following the same structure.

Finally, also provide number of clinical and sylistic edits. Number of edits should be described using the format <quantity> <definition> <unit>
Quantity should use the options: none=0, sparse=1-2, few=3-4, many=5+.
   => examples: "sparse (1-2) stylistic edits", "no clinical edits"

Requirements:
- Each descriptor should be $\leq$3 words.
- The descriptor should describe what the clinician DID to the draft, not what the note is about
- List specific terms, synonyms, and increasingly general concepts
- Separate items with commas
